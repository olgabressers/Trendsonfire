People & Transformation — Workforce Intelligence

US tech companies cut nearly 140,000 jobs in 2026. AI was cited in 33% of the cuts — up from 1% two years earlier.

US tech companies have cut nearly 140,000 jobs since the start of 2026, according to a Financial Times analysis reported by TechCrunch. AI was cited in 33% of 2026's tech layoff events, up from 1% in 2024, as Oracle, Meta, Amazon, Microsoft and dozens of other companies cut headcount while raising spending on AI. This report covers all 21 companies individually, including each company's own stated reason for the cuts.

01
2026 In Numbers
At least 94,046 US tech jobs were cut by August. May alone was the heaviest month since 2023.
US tech companies cut at least 94,046 jobs between January and August 2026, according to the Crunchbase Tech Layoffs Tracker — up 16.8% from 80,486 in the same period of 2025. May 2026 was the single heaviest month, with 31,513 job cuts, the highest monthly total since March 2023's 36,602. The pace then eased: June through August totaled 19,331 cuts, down 16.2% year on year. Large companies accounted for about 87% of everyone laid off in 2026, similar to 85% the year before.
Why the totals differ: Layoffs.fyi counts the picture differently from Crunchbase. On its own global count, Layoffs.fyi attributes 92,913 layoffs worldwide — 72% of this year's total on that tracker — to AI. Crunchbase's 94,046 figure covers US tech layoffs only, across all causes, not only AI-cited ones. The two trackers use different geography and a different methodology, so the totals are not directly comparable.
Sources: Crunchbase News, 25 Sept 2026, citing Layoffs.fyi founder Roger Lee.

The Full List
From Oracle's 21,000 to GitLab's 350: every company that named AI in a 2026 layoff
Twenty-one companies disclosed 2026 job cuts where AI was cited — by the company itself, by a named executive, or in a regulatory filing — as a factor. Sorted by announcement date. Where a company's own language did not tie the cuts directly to AI, that is noted rather than assumed.
Company Date, 2026 Jobs Cut Share of Workforce How AI Was Cited
Amazon 28 Jan 16,000 Part of a wider corporate reduction Official reason given was "reducing layers, increasing ownership, and removing bureaucracy" — no AI mention. CEO Andy Jassy had separately said generative AI and agents "should change the way our work is done."
Dell 30 Jan (disclosed Mar) 11,000 10% of headcount No AI cause was stated. The cuts came as Dell projected AI-optimized server revenue could double in fiscal 2027.
Salesforce 10 Feb <1,000 Marketing, product, data and the Agentforce AI unit Told Fortune that "because of the benefits and efficiencies of Agentforce, we've seen the number of support cases we handle decline." CEO Marc Benioff's "less heads" comment referred to an earlier, separate cut of about 4,000 customer-support roles.
Block 26–27 Feb 4,000 Nearly half the company CEO Jack Dorsey wrote that "intelligence tools we're creating and using" paired with "smaller and flatter teams" were behind the change, adding "most companies are late."
Oracle FY to 31 May (disclosed 22 Jun) 21,000 13% of headcount SEC filing: "the adoption and deployment of AI technologies across our operations have resulted" in workforce reductions, and may continue to.
Atlassian 11 Mar 1,600 10% CEO Mike Cannon-Brookes: "our approach is not 'AI replaces people,'" while adding "it would be disingenuous to pretend AI doesn't change the mix of skills we need."
IBM Rolling through 2026 Est. 3,000–9,000 (US) Part of a reduction exceeding 15,000 since Sept 2024 Bloomberg reported roughly 200 human-resources positions were replaced by AI agents. IBM said it is tripling entry-level hiring for AI and hybrid-cloud roles.
Snap 16 Apr 1,000 16% of global workforce CEO Evan Spiegel, in a filed memo: "rapid advancements in artificial intelligence enable our teams to reduce repetitive work."
Cloudflare 7–8 May 1,100 20% CEO Matthew Prince said most of those let go were in middle management, finance, legal and audit roles. No direct AI-attribution quote from the company.
Coinbase 5 May 700 14% CEO Brian Armstrong said engineers "use AI to ship in days what used to take a team weeks" and called for the company to "leverage AI across every facet of our jobs."
PayPal 5 May 4,500+ Part of a 20% reduction planned over 2–3 years CEO Enrique Lores told investors the company would "aggressively adopt AI" in its development processes and formed an internal "AI transformation and simplification" team.
Intuit 20 May 3,000 17% Reported as reallocating resources toward AI. CEO Sasan Goodarzi spoke of "reducing complexity" — no direct AI-attribution quote in company statements.
General Motors 12 May 500–600 Mostly technology roles GM's own statement cited "transforming its Information Technology organization," with no AI mention. A person familiar with the matter told CNBC "AI played a role in the decision," but not the only one.
Cisco 14 May 4,000 5% CFO Mark Patterson: "this was really not a savings-driven restructure," describing it as "realigning resources around silicon, optics, security and AI."
Meta 20–21 May 8,000 10% CEO Mark Zuckerberg said the cuts were necessary because "success isn't a given" in AI. Separately, about 7,000 employees already at Meta were moved into new AI-focused roles earlier in the year — a reallocation, not a 1-for-1 replacement of the roles cut.
Google Ongoing through May Est. 1,500–3,000+ No official company total disclosed Cuts came via performance reviews, a voluntary buyout and reorganizations. No direct AI-attribution quote from the company.
GitLab 3 Jun 350 14% CEO Bill Staples said agentic workloads are "pushing competitors to the brink," describing a "generational rebuild" of core infrastructure funded by the restructuring.
Microsoft 9 Jul 4,800 2.1% of global workforce The company said the roles were "not being replaced by AI," while acknowledging "AI is changing how work gets done." CFO Amy Hood cited building "high-performing teams that operate with pace and agility" amid rising AI investment.
Monday.com Late Jul Just over 600 About 20% SEC filing cited an "AI-driven growth strategy." Co-founder Eran Zinman's staff memo said the move "was not made to reduce costs or replace people with AI."
Barclays 22 Oct 5,000 About 5% of total workforce Q3 earnings presentation cited "organisational simplification and technology change efficiency driven by AI." About 60% of the cuts are expected in support functions, including developers.
Canva 3 Nov 1,200 22% (laid off or asked to relocate) CEO Melanie Perkins called it "the hardest moment in our 13-year history." Reporting ties the pressure to the cost of AI features Canva now provides and to competition from other AI tools; neither Perkins nor the reporting states that AI replaced the roles cut.

03
What Companies Actually Said
Only seven of the 21 companies said AI replaced the work. Ten said AI wasn't the reason — or said nothing at all.
Company language on AI and the cuts falls into three groups. Seven companies said, in their own statements, filings or an executive's own words, that AI was reducing the need for the roles cut. Four tied the cuts to the cost of AI investment or AI-related competitive pressure, without saying AI performed the work the cut roles did. Ten either said explicitly that AI was not the primary reason, or made no public statement connecting the cuts to AI at all — even though each appears on outside trackers of 2026's AI-linked layoffs.

04
How Analysts Read It
Andrew Challenger says AI is "literally" replacing jobs. Roger Lee says there's "little evidence" it is.
The two analysts who track layoff data most closely do not agree on how much of 2026's job cuts AI actually caused.
"There are jobs that are literally being replaced by artificial intelligence." … "They're letting people go from one area of their organization while they might even be hiring in an area that is focused on AI."Andrew Challenger, Challenger, Gray & Christmas — Crunchbase News, 25 September 2026
"There's been little evidence that AI is actually replacing the work of the human employees let go."Roger Lee, founder, Layoffs.fyi — on this year's largest AI-attributed layoffs, Crunchbase News, 25 September 2026
Where the two trackers disagree: Challenger, Gray & Christmas tracks announced US job cuts across every sector; Layoffs.fyi tracks tech-sector layoffs specifically and is the source behind the 33%/1% and 92,913-job figures above. Both are cited regularly by major business outlets. Neither claims the final word on how much of 2026's job cuts AI actually caused — a year into the AI-layoff narrative, the two people closest to the data still read it differently.
Source: Crunchbase News, Tech Layoffs Outpace 2025 As Big Companies Shift Spending To AI, 25 September 2026.
