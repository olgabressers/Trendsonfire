Interview Brief — General Manager, Netherlands (Commercial) · Shine Small Business Division, Cegid

Shine: 4 acquired brands become 1 Dutch P&L, inside a group that just agreed a €10 billion merger

Shine — the pan-European "financial copilot" for small businesses, now owned by French software group Cegid — is consolidating four separately-acquired Dutch brands (Shine NL, Employes, Tellow, Zervant) under one country commercial leader. The posting lands days after Cegid announced a €10 billion merger with payroll company Silae, the latest in a run of buy-and-build acquisitions.

Sources: cegid.com, businesswire.com, prnewswire.com, finextra.com, shine.co, globalbankingandfinance.com, official LinkedIn job posting Updated 9 September 2026
Key conclusions
The company
Shine serves 400,000+ small-business customers across France, Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands. Cegid (Silver Lake & KKR-backed, €967M FY2024 revenue) closed its acquisition of Shine on 7 June 2026, creating a combined reach of 1M+ SMBs and 15,000 accountants.
The role
Single P&L accountability for total Dutch revenue across 4 previously independent brands — Shine NL, Employes (payroll), Tellow (accounting), Zervant (invoicing) — each with its own founding history, acquisition date and unit economics. Full commercial budget control, blended customer-acquisition-cost accountability, and a mandate to build one go-to-market team out of four.
Timing
This posting sits inside a parent group mid-consolidation: Cegid announced a €10 billion merger with French payroll firm Silae on 9 September 2026 — the same week as this posting — creating a combined platform with roughly 2 million end customers and 13 million payslips processed monthly.
Key opportunity
The EU's mandatory e-invoicing reform (ViDA) takes effect for cross-border B2B transactions from 1 July 2030 — a genuine regulatory tailwind for every product Shine's Dutch brands sell. The commercial challenge is less "grow the market" and more "merge four go-to-market motions, each built for a different customer segment, into one coherent Dutch narrative."

Terms used on this page
CAC — Customer Acquisition Cost: total sales and marketing spend divided by new customers won in the period.
Blended CAC — a single acquisition-cost figure calculated across two or more product lines with different economics, rather than measured separately per line.
Payback period — how many months of a customer's revenue it takes to recover the cost of acquiring them.
LTV — Lifetime Value: the total revenue a customer is expected to generate before churning.
NRR — Net Revenue Retention: revenue retained and expanded from existing customers over a period, excluding new-customer revenue.
ARR — Annual Recurring Revenue: subscription revenue normalised to a yearly run rate.
ACV — Annual Contract Value: the average yearly value of a single customer contract.
GM — as used in this brief, General Manager: the role title itself, with full accountability for a market's commercial result.
ZZP — Zelfstandige Zonder Personeel: the Dutch term for a solo self-employed person with no staff.
MKB — Midden- en Kleinbedrijf: the Dutch term for small and medium-sized enterprises.
ViDA — VAT in the Digital Age: the EU reform package mandating structured e-invoicing and digital VAT reporting.
FTC — Fixed-Term Contract: an employment contract with a defined end date, as structured for this role (12 months, renewable across 3 years).

Interview-ready talking points
  1. Each brand has a genuinely different founding DNA — a French neobank spun out of a bank-owned platform, a Rabobank internal startup, two Dutch founders bootstrapping payroll software, and a Finnish invoicing company — which is exactly why "market coherence across all four brands" is named explicitly in the role, not assumed to happen on its own.
  2. Tellow and Employes were both acquired specifically to deepen Ageras/Shine's presence in the Netherlands — this is not a market the group entered opportunistically, it is one it has been building toward since 2020.
  3. The four brands currently serve overlapping but distinct segments — Tellow skews freelancer/ZZP, Employes and Shine NL skew small-employer, Zervant skews invoicing-only — which is the practical starting point for the "budget splits, local pricing and product prioritization" decisions the role owns.

The two self-employment counts measure different things and both are real: KVK counts registrations (still climbing), while CBS measures people whose main job is self-employment — falling since 1 January 2025, when the Dutch Tax Authority resumed stricter enforcement against "schijnzelfstandigheid" (sham self-employment). This directly touches Tellow's core freelancer customer base. Sources: CBS, ZZP Nieuws, Accountancy Vanmorgen.
Regulatory tailwind: ViDA e-invoicing
  1. The EU's VAT in the Digital Age package entered into force 14 April 2025.
  2. Structured e-invoicing (EN 16931 standard) becomes mandatory for cross-border intra-EU B2B and B2G supplies from 1 July 2030.
  3. Member states with existing domestic digital-reporting systems must align to the EU model by 1 January 2035.
  4. Cegid's own leadership has cited this reform directly as the strategic rationale for the Shine acquisition.
Interview-ready talking points
  1. A shrinking freelancer labour-force count and a growing e-invoicing compliance requirement pull in different directions — the commercial strategy has to lean into the compliance tailwind rather than assume freelancer volume growth alone will drive it.
  2. 2030 is a real deadline, not abstract regulation — it gives a Dutch commercial plan a genuine external forcing date to build toward.

Domain Knowledge
Unit economics for a multi-brand small-business roll-up
ConceptWhat it means for this role
Blended CACBecause the 4 brands run different acquisition motions (self-serve app download vs. accountant-channel referral vs. paid search), a single blended figure can mask which product is actually efficient — the posting's own language ("manages blended market customer acquisition cost") signals this is a known tension, not a solved problem.
Payback period disciplineFintech companies selling to small businesses face some of the steepest acquisition costs of any SaaS segment — efficient payback depends on picking the right channel per brand, not applying one playbook across all four.
Single-brand vs. multi-brand architectureThe core decision every roll-up faces: consolidate under one brand (maximises marketing efficiency, risks alienating each brand's existing customer base) or keep brands separate (preserves trust, duplicates go-to-market cost). The posting's ask for "a cohesive commercial strategy" across 4 kept brand names suggests the group has chosen the second path.
Why integration, not acquisition, is the failure pointBringing together businesses with different systems, cultures and pricing models is a leading cause of roll-up underperformance industry-wide — which is precisely the organisational-change mandate this role is built to own.
CAC/payback benchmarks: Aleph, Fatgraphs. Roll-up brand-architecture and integration-risk framing: Dealroom, Atin Studio.

The Job Description
General Manager, Netherlands (Commercial) — full posting, by section
Job title: General Manager, Netherlands (Commercial) · Location: Amsterdam · Seniority: Director · Employment: 12-month fixed-term contracts renewed across 3 consecutive years, leading to a permanent contract.
Responsibilities
  1. Owns total Dutch revenue against plan across all product lines.
  2. Controls the full market commercial budget and allocates it across product lines.
  3. Manages blended market customer acquisition cost and payback efficiency.
  4. Leads the local commercial team, country marketing, customer success and market partnerships.
  5. Develops market coherence across all four brands with a unified go-to-market narrative.
  6. Makes decisions on budget splits, local pricing, product prioritization and team hiring.
Requirements
  1. Proven track record owning market or business-unit revenue in B2B software, fintech or SaaS targeting small businesses or accountants.
  2. Real commercial budget responsibility with measurable customer-acquisition-cost and payback accountability.
  3. Experience managing multiple product lines or revenue streams simultaneously with different economic models.
  4. Demonstrated ability building or rebuilding commercial teams through organisational change.
  5. Background in PE-backed or post-merger environments.
  6. Native Dutch speaker and fluent English speaker, based in the Netherlands.
Source: official LinkedIn job posting.
