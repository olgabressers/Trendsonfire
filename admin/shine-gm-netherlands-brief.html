Interview Brief — General Manager, Netherlands (Commercial) · Shine, part of Cegid

Shine: 4 acquired brands become 1 Dutch P&L, days after a €10B parent-group merger

Shine, backed by Silver Lake, is consolidating a set of strong European brands into a single platform spanning banking, invoicing, accounting and payroll. The General Manager for the Netherlands owns how that comes together in this market.

Sources throughout: cegid.com, shine.co, official filings, LinkedIn job posting Updated 10 September 2026
Key conclusions
  • The company: Shine serves 400,000+ small businesses in France, Germany, Denmark and the Netherlands. Owned by Cegid (Silver Lake & KKR-backed, €967M FY2024 revenue) since 7 June 2026.
  • The role: One P&L across 4 brands with different products, founders and acquisition dates. Full budget control, blended customer-acquisition-cost accountability, one go-to-market team out of four.
  • The timing: Cegid announced a €10 billion merger with payroll firm Silae on 9 September 2026 — this role sits inside a group mid-consolidation at every level.
  • The opportunity: The EU's mandatory e-invoicing reform (ViDA) forces every Dutch small business to upgrade by 2030 — a built-in demand driver for all 4 products.

05
For The GM
The business challenges & opportunities that matter most
Challenges
  1. 4 brands, 4 different economics — no shared commercial playbook exists yet.
  2. Tellow's core freelancer base sits in a shrinking labour-force count (down 6 straight quarters).
  3. Blended CAC/payback across product lines is explicitly unsolved — named as open in the posting.
  4. Competing against a €2.8B-revenue incumbent (Visma) and the accountant-standard (Exact) with a smaller, newly-merged brand.
  5. Parent group itself mid-merger (Cegid–Silae, closing H1 2027) — attention and bandwidth risk.
Opportunities
  1. ViDA e-invoicing mandate (2030) forces every Dutch small business to upgrade — a built-in demand driver.
  2. Cross-sell across the installed base — 25,000+, 6,000+ and 150,000+ customers barely overlap today.
  3. Accountant channel already proven (300+ partner firms via Employes) — scalable distribution.
  4. KVK registrations still at record highs — new-business formation continues even as ZZP employment falls.
  5. First NL "financial copilot" bundling account, invoicing, accounting and payroll — no single rival offers all four natively.

The Job Description
General Manager, Netherlands (Commercial) — full posting, by section
Job title: General Manager, Netherlands (Commercial) · Location: Amsterdam · Seniority: Director · Employment: 12-month fixed-term contracts renewed across 3 consecutive years, leading to a permanent contract.
Responsibilities
  1. Owns total Dutch revenue against plan across all product lines.
  2. Controls the full market commercial budget and allocates it across product lines.
  3. Manages blended market customer acquisition cost and payback efficiency.
  4. Leads the local commercial team, country marketing, customer success and market partnerships.
  5. Develops market coherence across all four brands with a unified go-to-market narrative.
  6. Makes decisions on budget splits, local pricing, product prioritization and team hiring.
Requirements
  1. Proven track record owning market or business-unit revenue in B2B software, fintech or SaaS targeting small businesses or accountants.
  2. Real commercial budget responsibility with measurable customer-acquisition-cost and payback accountability.
  3. Experience managing multiple product lines or revenue streams simultaneously with different economic models.
  4. Demonstrated ability building or rebuilding commercial teams through organisational change.
  5. Background in PE-backed or post-merger environments.
  6. Native Dutch speaker and fluent English speaker, based in the Netherlands.
Source: official LinkedIn job posting.

Customer acquisition & efficiency
Blended customer acquisition cost
Acquisition cost calculated across all 4 product lines — the metric named directly in the posting.
Benchmark $150–400 for early-stage fintech targeting small businesses, $1,450–1,461 once at scale — the steepest CAC of any SaaS segment. On a €0–20/month subscription tier alone, without transaction/payroll fees layered in, CAC should sit closer to €50–150 (modelled estimate, not a published figure).
Acquisition-cost payback period
Months of customer revenue needed to recover the cost of acquiring them, tracked per brand and blended.
Benchmark SMB segment (under €15K annual contract value) recovers cost in 8–12 months; B2B SaaS median overall is 15–16 months.
Lifetime-value-to-acquisition-cost ratio
Total expected customer revenue against the cost of acquiring them — the standard efficiency check on growth spend.
Benchmark 3.0x or higher marks a healthy ratio for a subscription business.
Net revenue retention
Revenue retained and expanded from the existing Dutch customer base, excluding new logos.
Benchmark Small-business SaaS (under €25K annual contract value) retains around 97% on a median basis, versus roughly 118% for enterprise-focused SaaS.
