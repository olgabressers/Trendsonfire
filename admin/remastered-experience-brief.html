Positioning Strategy
Business-Market Interest — The Shift to an Affordable-Luxury Experience
The positioning shift is from a street-style, one-time attraction to an affordable-luxury destination — keeping the existing street-style audience while adding the comfort, service and story layer that brings in corporate groups, families and repeat visitors.
Reference case
House of Bols, Amsterdam
A single-venue, high-quality experience built on a tiered structure — a self-guided tour and tasting, a hosted cocktail workshop, a full-day academy course, and a private-events tier for corporate groups. The premium positioning comes from story (450 years of distillation heritage) and a hosted, service-led layer, not the visuals alone — a high-quality venue paired with a set of additional activities that draws the corporate community.
bols.com/pages/experience-tickets
Comfort & service — the layer the current experience is missing
Opportunity — seating for the full show
The current format has no seating for the ~60-minute show, a stretch for most visitors. Seating is a baseline comfort expectation at this length and price point.
Opportunity — refreshed facilities
Restroom facilities need refreshing to match a premium positioning and a corporate-hire audience.
Opportunity — a real beverage program
Drink options are currently basic. A hosted bar or beverage program — the House of Bols model — turns the venue into a longer, higher-spend visit.
Opportunity — a service-led model
On-site service is currently minimal. A hosted, service-forward layer is what separates an affordable-luxury destination from a walk-through attraction.
The shift is to affordable luxury, not a different audience — the existing street-style visitor base stays, joined by the corporate groups, families and repeat visitors that comfort, service and story bring in.
