Essential facts
  • Royal Philips: global health-technology company, HQ Amsterdam. FY2025 group sales €17.8B (+2% comparable), Adjusted EBITA margin 12.3% (+80bps). Q2 2026: group sales €4.4B (+4% comparable sales growth).
  • Chief Operations Officer Willem Appelo (since 2022) owns end-to-end operations, supply chain and manufacturing group-wide. Previously Vice President Supply Chain Strategy, Innovation & Deployment at Johnson & Johnson, and President of the Global Delivery and Technology Group at Xerox.
  • Productivity: €2.5B delivered 2023–2025 (€850M in 2025 alone). A new €1.5B program runs 2026–2028 — €258M delivered in the first half of 2026.
  • The role sits in Service Parts Supply Chain (SPS) within Service Parts Supply Chain for Philips Healthcare — the function that plans, resources and equips the parts network supporting every Philips Healthcare modality already installed in hospitals and clinics worldwide. Based in Best, Netherlands.
  • The posting explicitly names this "a newly formed global organization" — the mandate is to build the function, not run an existing one. Office-based teams work in-person at least 3 days a week.
Terms used on this page
SPS — Service Parts Supply Chain: the global Philips function that plans, warehouses, distributes and repairs the service parts supporting every Philips Healthcare device already installed and in active use.
ISC — Integrated Supply Chain: Philips' central group covering manufacturing, supply chain and procurement across the end-to-end "plan – source – make – deliver – install" value stream for new equipment. SPS is the parallel function for equipment already installed.
Installed base — the total stock of Philips equipment already sold and running at hospitals and clinics; the base SPS exists to keep in service.
EBITA — Earnings Before Interest, Tax and Amortisation: Philips' primary profitability metric.
Comparable sales growth — Philips' adjusted revenue-growth metric, stripping out currency effects and portfolio changes (acquisitions/divestments) to isolate underlying performance.
Center of Excellence (CoE) — a centralized team that sets standards and best practice for a function group-wide; the posting names both a Supply Chain CoE and a Service CoE as key partners.
OTIF — On Time In Full: the share of parts delivered on the promised date and in the complete quantity requested.
Fill rate — the share of part requests met immediately from stock on hand, without backorder or delay.
FTE — Full-Time Equivalent: a standardised way of counting headcount.

KPIs
KPI framework — by function
Parts availability & fulfillment
Fill rate
Share of part requests met immediately from stock on hand, without backorder or delay.
Benchmark A fill rate above 95% is the standard target for consistent field-service fulfillment.
OTIF (on time, in full)
Parts delivered on the promised date and in the complete quantity requested.
Benchmark Core fulfillment KPI across supply-chain functions; tracked alongside fill rate since one can always be bought at the expense of the other by holding more stock.
Emergency / expedited shipment rate
Share of part requests resolved through emergency procurement or expedited transfer rather than planned stock.
Benchmark One of the core inventory-risk indicators service-parts teams are advised to track first.
Field-service downtime linked to parts
Equipment downtime at hospitals/clinics attributable to parts unavailability rather than the repair itself.
Benchmark A standard field-service KPI wherever parts availability directly gates equipment uptime.
Forecasting & inventory
Forecast accuracy
How closely demand planning for service parts matches actual consumption.
Benchmark 85–90% accuracy is a common target range for demand forecasting.
Inventory turnover
How many times service-parts inventory is sold/used and replaced over a period — the standard counterweight to fill rate.
Benchmark One of the five core inventory KPIs every supply-chain planner is advised to track.
Days of inventory outstanding
Average number of days parts sit in stock before use.
Benchmark Tracked alongside turnover as the standard inventory-efficiency pair.
Stock-out rate
Share of part requests that hit zero available stock at the point of need.
Benchmark A direct downstream signal of forecast-accuracy and inventory-turnover performance.
AI & data adoption
AI use cases in production
Number of identified AI use cases that have moved from pilot to adopted, in-production tooling — the posting's own "beyond proof-of-concept" bar.
Benchmark Internally defined against SPS's own use-case pipeline — no external adoption-rate standard applies across companies.
Data architecture coverage
Share of SPS processes running on the target data architecture versus legacy/manual structures.
Benchmark Internally defined — tied to the specific architecture design principles this role owns.
Time-to-decision on IT investment requests
Speed of the annual IT investment-planning process this role drives, from request to funded project.
Benchmark Internally defined against Philips' own planning cycle.
Analytics/BI tool adoption by planners
Share of SPS planning and analytics staff actively using the tools this role builds, versus legacy spreadsheet-based work.
