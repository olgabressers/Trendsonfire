The venues

Eleven venues across seven countries. They are not eleven independent businesses — MINA alone operates five of them, so the set represents six operators.

  • Six operators, not eleven. MINA runs Bucharest, Cluj, Iași, Timișoara and Sofia. Any comparison that counts venues rather than operators overstates the number of parties in the room.
  • Only two venues publish their floor area. Visiodrom at 6,500 m² and OASIS at 2,200 m². Nobody else discloses it, so size-normalised comparison is impossible from outside.
  • Three of the eleven opened within the last eighteen months. GIOLABS, MINA Sofia and MINA Iași. A third of the set has no trading history to benchmark.

Social media

Audience and published content volume across five platforms, captured 12 September 2026.

  • Remastered has the smallest YouTube presence in the entire set — 47 subscribers and 14 videos. Kunstkraftwerk has 553 subscribers across 103 videos; MINA 217 across 159; Sensea 1,740 across 25. For a business whose product is moving image, that is the most conspicuous gap on this page.
  • On TikTok, Remastered is second in the set by followers — 6,870, ahead of OASIS at 6,065 and behind MINA Bucharest at 12,300.
  • But its engagement is far weaker. Remastered has 69,500 likes against OASIS's 185,200 — from a slightly larger follower base. Per follower, OASIS earns 30.5 likes to Remastered's 10.1.
  • Only three of the six operators have a findable TikTok at all — Remastered, MINA and OASIS. Kunstkraftwerk, Visiodrom, GIOLABS and Sensea have none.
  • On Instagram, Remastered sits mid-table at 20,000 — behind MINA Bucharest (53,000), Sensea (45,000), Kunstkraftwerk (37,000) and OASIS (30,000), ahead of Visiodrom (15,000).
  • And it publishes less. 655 Instagram posts against MINA Bucharest's 1,186 and Kunstkraftwerk's 1,103 — roughly half the content of the two largest accounts in the set.

Price architecture

Every published rate, converted at the September 2026 reference rates of €1 = CAD 1.602 and €1 = RON 5.25.

  • Remastered has the highest headline price in the set at €24.50, in a range running from roughly €9.50 to €21.82.
  • It is not the only venue adding charges on top. Remastered adds €1.45 per ticket in service fees; OASIS states its prices exclude taxes and fees. Every other venue publishes an all-in price.

Section 6

Opening hours and capacity

Seats per time slot are private everywhere in this set except Remastered. Opening hours are public everywhere, and they are the one capacity measure that can be compared across all venues.

  • Remastered opens seven days a week and still ends up mid-table on hours. 40.5 hours across 7 days, against Kunstkraftwerk's 36 hours across only 4.
  • Three of its seven days are 4.5-hour days. Monday, Tuesday and Thursday close at 15:00. A 4.5-hour trading day carries nearly the full fixed cost of opening — staffing, projection, climate, cleaning — for 60% of the trading window.
  • GIOLABS, the newest venue in the set, trades the longest week — 47 hours across 6 days.
  • Kunstkraftwerk concentrates. Four days, 36 hours, an average 9-hour day, and it closes Monday to Wednesday entirely rather than opening thin.
  • Remastered's average trading day is 5.8 hours — the shortest in the set. Kunstkraftwerk averages 9.0, GIOLABS 7.8, OASIS 7.9, Visiodrom 8.4.

Section 7

Modelled revenue

No venue in this set publishes revenue or visitors. What follows is a model built on public inputs, designed to compare venues against each other on a single consistent basis — not to estimate any individual venue's actual turnover.

Method, stated in full

Step 1. Annual operating hours = published hours per week × 52.

Step 2. One throughput assumption, applied identically to every venue: 46 visitors per operating hour. It is derived from Remastered's own publicly confirmed milestones — 200,000 visitors by June 2023 and 500,000 by July 2026, an average pace of about 97,000 a year — divided by its 2,106 annual operating hours.

Step 3. Modelled revenue = modelled visitors × the venue's published adult price.

What this deliberately does not do. It does not vary throughput by venue, because no public data supports doing so. It uses the adult price for every visitor, which overstates every venue equally — children, groups, schools and discount cards all pull the real blended price below it. It excludes every non-ticket revenue line. It is a relative instrument, and the ranking it produces is the output, not the euro figures.

  • Remastered models highest in the set at €2.37m — and price is doing all of the work. Its operating hours are third of five and its throughput is the model's constant, so the entire gap to the others comes from charging €24.50.
  • GIOLABS trades 16% more hours than Remastered and models 24% below it. That is the price gap made visible.
  • Kunstkraftwerk models lowest — the fewest operating hours in the set, at a price a third below Rotterdam's.
  • The spread across the whole set is only €1.55m to €2.37m. On these inputs, no venue is operating at a fundamentally different scale from any other. The differences are price and opening hours, not size.

Section 8

Revenue streams operated

Which income lines each operator actually runs, as advertised on its own website. Public data cannot say what each stream earns — it can say with confidence whether the venue runs it at all.

  • Visiodrom operates the widest mix — restaurant, shop, a €49.00 annual pass, a €6.00 school rate, and five cross-attraction combination tickets with a zoo and three museums.
  • Kunstkraftwerk is the most commercially inventive. Its own link hub sells a membership, an exclusive event location, immersive yoga, a corporate Christmas party product and a Mobile Immersive Box — the show taken out of the building and sold elsewhere.
  • Only two operators sell a membership or annual pass. Visiodrom and Kunstkraftwerk. It is the most direct instrument a venue has for repeat visitation and most of the set does not offer one.
  • Only one operator sells combination tickets with neighbouring attractions. Visiodrom, with five partners. Everyone else sells only itself.
  • Every operator runs corporate or private hire. OASIS states "more than 150 corporate, institutional, and private events" since opening. It is the one stream the whole category agrees on.
  • Remastered publicly advertises the narrowest mix in the set — tickets, event hire, gift vouchers and a pilates session. No published school rate, no membership, no combination ticket, no café or shop on its own site.

Section 9

The cost half — where public data stops

A benchmark on operations and finance has two halves. Everything above is the first half. This is the second, and public data delivers none of it.

Not one venue in this set publishes any of the following

Every venue here is privately held, several in jurisdictions where small companies file abbreviated accounts with no profit and loss statement at all. Nothing below can be obtained without the operator handing it over:

  • Visitor numbers, in total or by month
  • Revenue, in total or split by stream
  • Seats per time slot and slots per day — the real capacity number
  • Staff cost, headcount, and the split between fixed and flexible labour
  • Rent, and whether the building is leased or owned
  • Content cost — produced in-house, co-produced or licensed, and on what terms
  • Projection and technology capital expenditure, and the replacement cycle
  • Marketing spend, and the split between brand and performance
  • Reseller and online travel agent commission, and the direct-to-reseller channel mix
  • Capacity utilisation and occupancy
  • Secondary spend per visitor, contribution margin and EBITDA
  • The public half is finished, in a day, at no cost. Everything in sections 1 to 8 came from published sources and took one working session.
  • The private half cannot be started at any price without operator consent. No amount of desk research substitutes for a venue opening its accounts.
  • So what a paid benchmark actually sells is access, not analysis — persuading operators to submit comparable data, and defining the template that makes the submissions comparable.
  • Which means the template should exist before anyone is asked for a number. Common definitions of visitor, capacity, occupancy, contribution, and the boundary between content cost and capital expenditure. Without them, six operators will submit six incompatible spreadsheets.
