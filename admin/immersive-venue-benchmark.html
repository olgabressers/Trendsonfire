Immersive venue benchmark · public data only

A benchmark of ten immersive art venues — built entirely from public data

A standard venue benchmark asks two questions: how do comparable venues differ on operations and finance, and how does one venue perform against the strongest in the set. This page runs that scope as far as public data allows — price by price, minute by minute, revenue stream by revenue stream — and then marks precisely where public data stops and member cooperation becomes the only way forward.

12 September 2026 · 10 venues across 7 countries · every figure below was fetched from the venue's own website or a named public source

Method — what this page can and cannot establish

Established from public sources
  • Ticket price and full price architecture — child, reduced, group, family, school
  • Service and booking fees charged on top of the headline price
  • Show and visit duration, where the venue publishes it
  • Opening days and hours
  • Which revenue streams the venue actually operates
  • Memberships, annual passes and cross-attraction combination tickets
  • Floor area, where the venue publishes it
Not obtainable from public sources
  • Visitor numbers — none of these venues publishes them
  • Revenue, in total or by stream
  • Every cost category: staff, rent, content, technology, marketing
  • Contribution margin, EBITDA, profitability
  • Capacity utilisation and occupancy
  • Secondary spend per visitor
  • Channel mix and commission paid to resellers

Section 1

The comparison set

Ten immersive art venues in seven countries. Three of them — OASIS immersion, Kunstkraftwerk and Remastered — are the publicly named founding members of the Immersive Center Alliance, formed at NUMIX LAB 2024.

  • The set is not ten independent operators. MINA alone runs five venues — Bucharest, Cluj, Iași, Timișoara and Sofia — so ten venues represent six operators.
  • Only two venues publish their floor area. Visiodrom at 6,500 m² and OASIS at 2,200 m². The rest do not disclose it, which means any size-normalised comparison is already impossible from outside.
  • Opening hours vary from four days a week to seven. Kunstkraftwerk opens Thursday to Sunday; GIOLABS Tuesday to Sunday; Visiodrom Wednesday to Sunday. Available trading days differ by up to 40% before a single visitor is counted.

Section 2

Price benchmark

Standard adult admission, as published by each venue, converted at the September 2026 reference rates of €1 = CAD 1.602 and €1 = RON 5.25.

  • Remastered is the most expensive venue in the set — €24.50, against a set that runs from roughly €9.50 to €20.
  • Remastered is the only venue that adds a service fee on top of the headline price. €1.45 per ticket, bringing the real adult price to €25.95. No other venue in the set publishes a fee of any kind on its own website.
  • The price architecture is thinner. Visiodrom publishes eight price categories including a €6.00 school rate and a €39.00 family ticket; Remastered publishes two.
  • The gap to the cheapest is 2.7×. MINA charges RON 50 — about €9.50 — for a venue in a capital city of 1.7 million people.

Section 3

Price per minute of experience

The comparison that matters more than the headline price: what a visitor pays for each minute inside the venue. Shown only for the four venues that publish both a price and a duration.

  • Remastered charges the most per minute in the set, by a wide margin — €0.41, rising to €0.43 once the service fee is counted.
  • That is 2.4 times Kunstkraftwerk and 1.9 times OASIS. Both are larger venues in larger cities.
  • Remastered also runs the shortest experience of the four — 60 minutes, against 70–80 at OASIS and a recommended 90–120 at Kunstkraftwerk.
  • Highest price, shortest visit. That combination is visible to any visitor comparing venues online, and it is the single clearest finding public data produces.

Section 4

Revenue streams operated

Which income lines each venue actually runs, as advertised on its own website. This is the one half of a finance benchmark that public data can genuinely answer — not how much each stream earns, but whether the venue operates it at all.

  • Visiodrom operates the widest mix in the set — restaurant, shop, a €49.00 annual pass, a school rate, and five cross-attraction combination tickets with a zoo, three museums and a second attraction.
  • Only two venues publicly sell a membership or annual pass. Visiodrom and Kunstkraftwerk. An annual pass is the most direct instrument a venue has for repeat visitation, and most of the set does not offer one.
  • Only one venue sells combination tickets with neighbouring attractions. Visiodrom, with five partners at €22.00–€29.00. Everyone else sells only itself.
  • Every venue in the set runs corporate or private hire. OASIS states "more than 150 corporate, institutional, and private events" since opening; GIOLABS privatises five rooms; MINA and Remastered both publish an events page. It is the one stream the whole category agrees on.
  • Alternative programming is spreading. MINA runs immersive yoga, jazz concerts and screenings; GIOLABS and Remastered both run immersive pilates. Same idea, arrived at independently.

Section 5

The cost half — why a public benchmark stops here

A benchmark on "operations and finance" has two halves. Public data delivers the first. It delivers none of the second.

Zero of these venues publishes any of the following

Every venue in this set is a privately held company. Several sit in jurisdictions where small companies file abbreviated accounts with no profit and loss statement at all. Nothing below can be obtained without the venue handing it over:

  • Visitor numbers, in total or by month
  • Revenue, in total or split by stream
  • Staff cost, headcount, and the split between fixed and flexible labour
  • Rent, and whether the building is leased or owned
  • Content cost — produced in-house, co-produced, or licensed, and on what terms
  • Technology and projection capital expenditure, and the replacement cycle
  • Marketing spend, and the split between brand and performance
  • Reseller and online travel agent commission, and the direct-to-reseller channel mix
  • Capacity, utilisation and occupancy
  • Secondary spend per visitor
  • Contribution margin and EBITDA
  • The public half is finished, in a day, at no cost. Everything in sections 1 to 4 came from published sources.
  • The private half cannot be started at any price without member consent. No amount of desk research substitutes for a venue agreeing to open its accounts.
  • So the real deliverable of a paid benchmark is not analysis — it is access. The value sits in persuading operators to submit comparable data on a common template, and in defining that template so the submissions can actually be compared.
  • Which means the template is the first thing that should exist, before anyone is asked for a number. Common definitions of visitor, capacity, occupancy, contribution, and the boundary between content cost and capital expenditure.
