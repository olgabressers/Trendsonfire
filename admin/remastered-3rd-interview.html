Equity — the options, and which one protects me

My money should not depend on their decision to sell. In an NL BV, real shares make me a minority shareholder in a private company — illiquid, dilutable, and paid only on an exit I don't control. There are cleaner structures.
StructureWhat it isUpsideThe catch for me
Real shares
(aandelen)		Actual ownership in the BV. Moves by notarial deed.Voting, dividend, capital gain on a sale.Illiquid minority; pays only if they sell; dilutable; I become an insider with duties.
Certificaten
(via STAK)		Economic rights only, voting stays with the foundation.Dividend + gain, without governance drag.Same illiquidity — still sale-dependent.
Share options
(opties)		Right to buy shares later at a set price.Leveraged upside if value rises.Worth something only on a rise and a sale; taxed at exercise.
Phantom / SARCash that tracks share value. No ownership, no notary.Full economic upside; taxed as salary.Only safe with a guaranteed put / cash-out trigger — otherwise still their call.
Profit-share
(winstdeling / tantième)		A % of annual profit, contractual.Paid every year, regardless of a sale — I control it via performance.Taxed as salary; no ownership or governance rights.
What I'd take: a profit-share plus a growth bonus I trigger myself, or phantom equity with a guaranteed cash-out — never plain minority shares that pay only on an exit I don't control.
In an NL BV, real shares transfer by notarial deed and make me a shareholder with insider duties and dilution risk; phantom / SAR and profit-share are contractual cash, taxed as salary, with no ownership. Have the equity terms and the management agreement reviewed by a Dutch corporate / employment lawyer before signing.

What I'm on the hook for — GM responsibility & liability

One question decides my personal exposure: am I appointed statutair bestuurder (statutory director, registered at the KvK and formally on the board) or titulair directeur (director in title, but an employee)? These carry completely different liability.
If I am…My personal liability
Titulair directeur
(employee with a director title)		Normal employee liability only — personally liable to the company only for intent or deliberate recklessness (art. 7:661 BW). Low personal exposure.
Statutair bestuurder
(on the board, KvK-registered)		Real personal liability: to the company for improper management (art. 2:9 BW); in bankruptcy, if management was manifestly improper and a main cause (art. 2:248 BW) — with late-filed accounts or poor bookkeeping creating a presumption against me; personally & jointly for unpaid payroll tax / VAT if the company fails to file a timely inability-to-pay notice (betalingsonmacht); and to third parties in tort for taking on obligations the company can't meet (Beklamel).
What I require before signing — whichever applies
  • D&O insurance in force (bestuurdersaansprakelijkheidsverzekering) with adequate cover — and I see the policy.
  • Indemnity (vrijwaring) from the company in the management agreement, for claims arising from the proper performance of my role.
  • Clear decision rights & board minutes — capex, pricing and hiring approvals documented, so I'm not personally exposed for shareholder decisions.
  • Annual décharge (formal discharge) granted each year.
  • Proper bookkeeping & timely KvK filing already in place — this is itself a personal-liability trigger, and it ties straight back to the health check above.
Director liability bites hardest in insolvency — which is exactly why I won't become a statutair bestuurder of a company whose real numbers I haven't seen. This is the framework to raise with them and to verify with a Dutch corporate lawyer; it is not legal advice to rely on. Have the managementovereenkomst / arbeidsovereenkomst reviewed before you sign.
