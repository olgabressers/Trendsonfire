The cost of growth — the number that has to hold

Reaching 125,000 means buying 28,000 more visits. Each is worth €19.63 in contribution — so the cost of each extra visit must stay under €19.63, or growth loses money. The target: 112,000 visitors profitably, not 125,000 at any cost. The 28,000 come from free levers first (conversion, yield, off-peak fill), repeat rate (founder-dependent), then B2B — not 28,000 bought clicks.

Equity — the options, and which one fits

Compensation shouldn't hinge on a future sale the shareholders control. In a Dutch BV, real shares mean a minority stake in a private company — illiquid, dilutable, and paid only on an exit. There are cleaner structures.
StructureWhat it isUpsideThe catch
Real shares
(aandelen)		Actual ownership in the BV. Moves by notarial deed.Voting, dividend, capital gain on a sale.Illiquid minority; pays only on a sale; dilutable; the holder becomes an insider with duties.
Certificaten
(via STAK)		Economic rights only, voting stays with the foundation.Dividend + gain, without governance drag.Same illiquidity — still sale-dependent.
Share options
(opties)		Right to buy shares later at a set price.Leveraged upside if value rises.Worth something only on a rise and a sale; taxed at exercise.
Phantom shares
(cash-settled)		Cash that tracks share value. No ownership, no notary.Full economic upside; taxed as salary.Sound only with a guaranteed cash-out trigger — otherwise still sale-dependent.
Profit-share
(winstdeling / tantième)		A % of annual profit, contractual.Paid every year, regardless of a sale — driven by performance.Taxed as salary; no ownership or governance rights.
Best fit: a profit-share plus a performance-linked growth bonus, or phantom equity with a guaranteed cash-out — rather than minority shares that pay only on an exit.
In a Dutch BV, real shares transfer by notarial deed and make the holder a shareholder with insider duties and dilution risk; phantom shares and profit-share are contractual cash, taxed as salary, with no ownership. Have the equity terms and the management agreement reviewed by a Dutch corporate / employment lawyer before signing.

General-manager responsibility & personal liability

One question decides the personal exposure: is the appointment a statutair bestuurder (statutory director, registered at the Chamber of Commerce and formally on the board) or a titulair directeur (director in title, but an employee)? The two carry completely different liability.
AppointmentPersonal liability
Titulair directeur
(employee with a director title)		Normal employee liability only — personally liable to the company only for intent or deliberate recklessness (art. 7:661 Dutch Civil Code). Low personal exposure.
Statutair bestuurder
(on the board, Chamber-of-Commerce-registered)		Real personal liability: to the company for improper management (art. 2:9 Dutch Civil Code); in bankruptcy, if management was manifestly improper and a main cause (art. 2:248) — with late-filed accounts or poor bookkeeping creating a presumption against the director; personally & jointly for unpaid payroll tax / VAT if the company fails to file a timely inability-to-pay notice (betalingsonmacht); and to third parties in tort for taking on obligations the company can't meet (Beklamel).
What to secure before signing — whichever applies
  • Directors' & officers' liability insurance in force (bestuurdersaansprakelijkheidsverzekering) with adequate cover — policy confirmed.
  • Indemnity (vrijwaring) from the company in the management agreement, for claims arising from the proper performance of the role.
  • Clear decision rights & board minutes — capital spend, pricing and hiring approvals documented, so the director isn't personally exposed for shareholder decisions.
  • Annual décharge (formal discharge) granted each year.
  • Proper bookkeeping & timely Chamber-of-Commerce filing already in place — itself a personal-liability trigger, and it ties straight back to the health questions above.
Director liability bites hardest in insolvency — which is why the financial-health questions above matter before accepting a statutair bestuurder appointment. This is the framework to raise and to verify with a Dutch corporate lawyer; it is not legal advice to rely on. Have the managementovereenkomst / arbeidsovereenkomst reviewed before signing.
