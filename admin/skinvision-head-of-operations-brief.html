SkinVision: a Class IIa skin-cancer device with six operational areas to run as one

Founded in Amsterdam in 2012, SkinVision runs an artificial intelligence skin-cancer risk assessment certified as a Class IIa medical device, with more than 90% sensitivity and 89% specificity. Nearly 3 million people have used it, the image database holds more than 6 million skin images, and insurer partnerships can reach 20 million people. High-risk results are reviewed by dermatologists. Operations sits on that clinical pathway: support, clinical review, care access, billing, tooling and service analytics. The Head of Operations reports to the chief executive officer and owns all six.

Sources: SkinVision pricing page, SkinVision LinkedIn job description, SkinVision Head of Operations role description, skinvision.com, Halldale (28 January 2019) Updated 18 October 2026

Role Analysis
3 structural challenges this role is built to solve
01
Six areas, one dataset
  • Support, clinical review, care access, billing, tooling and analytics run semi-independently, each small in headcount and wide in subject matter
  • Each team sees its own slice below the noise threshold; the pooled, stratified view is where real problems show
02
Specificity gains with a sensitivity floor
  • The algorithm is tuned for high sensitivity, so human review recovers specificity by reducing false positives
  • Sensitivity is a floor and is only reported alongside specificity held constant; any movement is an immediate escalation to Clinical and Regulatory
03
You cannot stratify what you do not collect
  • Device model, operating system, age band, market, language and self-reported skin type need consistent, joinable capture before any analysis
  • Smaller strata, such as darker skin types in a majority-European user base, are the hardest to measure; "we cannot yet tell" is reported as a status

EU Medical Device Regulation 2017/745: complaint handling and vigilance
TopicWhat the regulation saysWhat it means for Operations
Incident (Article 2)Any malfunction or deterioration in the characteristics or performance of a device, including use-error due to ergonomic features, any inadequacy in the information supplied by the manufacturer, and any undesirable side-effectA support contact about confusing guidance text or a wrong result can be an incident; this is why every contact is classified at capture
Serious incident (Article 2)An incident that directly or indirectly led, might have led or might lead to death, serious deterioration of health, or a serious public health threatContacts classified as suspected incidents or clinically urgent go to Regulatory with a documented handoff time
Reporting deadlines (Article 87)Report once a causal relationship is established or reasonably possible, no later than 15 days after becoming aware. Serious public health threat: immediately, no later than 2 days. Death or unanticipated serious deterioration: no later than 10 days. An incomplete initial report may be followed by a complete one. If the manufacturer is uncertain whether an incident is reportable, it still reports within the deadlineThe clock starts when the manufacturer becomes aware, so the time a contact was received and the time it reached Regulatory are the evidence that matters. The determination of reportability stays with Regulatory
Trend reporting (Article 88)Any statistically significant increase in the frequency or severity of incidents that are not serious incidents, or of expected undesirable side-effects, that could affect the benefit-risk analysis, reported through the electronic system. The method and observation period are set in the post-market surveillance planContact volumes by category and segment, kept consistent over time, are the raw data for trend analysis
Post-market surveillance (Article 83)A system, part of the quality management system, that actively and systematically gathers, records and analyses data on quality, performance and safety throughout the device lifetime and feeds benefit-risk updates, usability improvements and preventive or corrective actionsSupport, review and follow-up data are inputs to this system; the unified, stratified view in the mandate is that input
Field safety corrective action (Article 2)Corrective action by a manufacturer for technical or medical reasons to prevent or reduce the risk of a serious incident; the communication to users is a field safety noticeOperations carries out the user communication, workarounds and post-incident records handed to Regulatory, as described for Business Systems in the briefing
Sources: Article 87 · Article 88 · Article 83 · Article 2 definitions, Advisera reproduction of Regulation (EU) 2017/745.

General Data Protection Regulation: special-category health data, consent and reuse of outcome data
TopicWhat the regulation saysWhat it means for Operations
Data concerning health (Article 4)Personal data related to the physical or mental health of a person, including the provision of health care services, which reveal information about health statusSkin images, results, referrals and follow-up outcomes all qualify
Special categories (Article 9)Processing of data concerning health is prohibited unless an exception applies; one exception is explicit consent to the processing for one or more specified purposesOutcome capture after a high-risk result is consent-dependent, as the role description states
Consent (Articles 4 and 7)Freely given, specific, informed and unambiguous. The controller must be able to demonstrate consent; the request must be clearly distinguishable, in plain language; consent can be withdrawn at any time and withdrawing must be as easy as giving it; the person is informed before consentingConsent records, wording by market, and a working withdrawal route are operational deliverables
Purpose limitation (Article 5)Data is collected for specified, explicit and legitimate purposes and not further processed in an incompatible manner; further processing for scientific research or statistical purposes is not considered incompatible, subject to Article 89 safeguardsThis is the rule behind the role description's hard limit: use beyond the original purpose goes to Legal, Regulatory and the consent basis first
Reuse for another purpose (Article 6)Without consent or a legal basis, the controller assesses compatibility by weighing the link between purposes, the context of collection, the nature of the data including special categories, the consequences for the person, and safeguardsReusing outcome data for evidence dossiers or partner business cases needs this assessment before any commercial conversation
Research and statistics safeguards (Article 89)Technical and organisational measures that respect data minimisation, which may include pseudonymisation; where the purpose can be met without identifying people, it is met that wayAggregated, de-identified outcome evidence is the form the briefing allows
