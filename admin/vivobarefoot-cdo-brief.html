Interview Brief — Chief Digital Officer · Executive Team

Vivobarefoot: £91.4M, 70% digital, two years of losses

Turnover £91.4M, up 5% — but a second consecutive year of operating losses, an EBITDA margin that has fallen from +2.0% to -2.4% in two years, and the historically largest market, North America, down 10%. Over 70% of revenue already runs through digital channels. The newly created Chief Digital Officer seat sits on the Executive Team, owns ecommerce strategy, technology and data infrastructure, and reports directly into fixing all three.

Sources: Vivobarefoot Integrated Annual Report 2024/25, LinkedIn job posting, Companies House, Sofina Group Updated 9 September 2026
Key conclusions
The company
Vivobarefoot is a certified B Corp barefoot footwear company headquartered in Bristol, UK, founded by brothers Galahad and Asher Clark. Turnover reached £91.4M in the 52 weeks to 28 June 2025, up 5% on £87.2M the year before, on 1.2M pairs sold (+12%). Sold internationally via ecommerce, distributors and wholesale; manufactured in Portugal, Namibia, Mongolia and Vietnam.
The role
Chief Digital Officer, sitting on the Executive Team, based in Bristol. Owns global ecommerce strategy and profit & loss, the Ecommerce Centre of Excellence, technology architecture, CRM and data infrastructure, and AI-led digital transformation. Requires proven ownership of ecommerce revenues exceeding £50M+.
Financial context
A second consecutive year of operating losses (-£2.9M FY24/25, net loss £3.3M). EBITDA margin has moved from +2.0% (FY23) to -0.7% (FY24) to -2.4% (FY25). Ecommerce contribution margin has slipped from 28% to 26.0% over the same period, even as digital carries over 70% of total revenue.
Key opportunity
North America — historically the main growth engine — declined 10% to £33.1M while Rest of World grew 53%. Distribution & Wholesale returned to double-digit growth (+14% in pairs) while the company's own Marketplace channel is named internally as underserved. The mandate: one Executive Team owner across ecommerce, technology and data to turn digital scale into digital profit.

Is this good or bad?
The ecommerce contribution margin (26.0%) is fine — it sits inside the normal 22–30% range for a direct-to-consumer brand this size. The EBITDA margin (-2.4%) is the real problem: the footwear industry averages +13.6%, and strong operators (On Holding, Deckers) run 17–24%. Vivobarefoot is roughly 16–26 percentage points below where a healthy footwear brand should sit — a genuine profitability gap, not just a rounding issue.
Why two years of losses — in the company's own words
The accounting bridge — the headline number overstates the trading decline
The underlying result before depreciation, share options and the Revivo provision was -£0.5M FY24/25, only a modest decline from +£0.4M FY23/24. The reported -£2.9M operating loss also carries depreciation (-£0.8M), a share-option fair-value charge (-£0.4M), and a -£1.6M increase in the Revivo inventory provision — a one-off policy change to fully write off the original cost of returned shoes resold via Revivo.com, which the company states gives "a more accurate and fair view of Revivo profitability."
The operational causes the Founders cite directly
Continued supply chain disruption; the impact of US tariffs on consumer confidence; a challenging warehouse move in Europe; failing to effectively serve the Marketplace channel; and weak new-customer growth in the US, the company's most important market. The Founders state these headwinds "curtailed our topline growth but also stifled profitability, resulting in a second year of operating losses."
Three of the five named causes — the Marketplace channel, US new-customer growth, and the systems behind a smoother warehouse/fulfilment transition — sit directly inside this role's remit.
Source: Vivobarefoot Integrated Annual Report 2024/25, Founders' financial update and notes to the financial statements (operating loss note).

Ownership & Governance
Who owns and governs Vivobarefoot
Founder control
Galahad Clark, Co-Founder & CEO, holds more than 25% but not more than 50% of shares and voting rights — the company's largest disclosed single stake. Asher Clark, Co-Founder, holds the right to appoint or remove directors. Both are seventh-generation cobblers from the Clark family (of Clarks shoes); the two businesses are entirely separate today.
Institutional investor
Sofina, a family-controlled investment company listed on Euronext Brussels, acquired a 14.35% shareholding in 2024 and holds an observer seat in Vivobarefoot's decision-making bodies — a minority, non-controlling position.
Disclosure note
The remainder of the capitalisation table beyond these two disclosed holdings is not published in Companies House filings or on Sofina's own site. Do not state a precise ownership split beyond what is confirmed here.
