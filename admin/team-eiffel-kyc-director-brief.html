Essential facts
  • Dutch interim/consultancy/project-management group, founded 1992 by Ferdi van Dommelen. Holding entity Equipe Holdings 3 B.V., HQ Marathon 4, Hilversum.
  • CEO since 9 Feb 2026: Jo Maes (ex-CEO of Ordina and Sopra Steria), replacing Gert-Jan Meppelink. CFO: Yke Bonenberg.
  • 2025 was a down year group-wide: revenue roughly flat, EBITDA fell 20-21%, net loss €38.4M, driven by lower billability and a cautious Dutch market.
  • The Financial Services & Compliance segment — where the KYC Center sits — was the worst-performing of the four: revenue down, EBITDA thinnest of any segment (6.1% margin), and a €5.1M goodwill impairment booked in 2025 due to "continued challenging market conditions" and pricing pressure.
  • From 2026, Team EIFFEL restructures from 4 business lines to 3, folding Financial Services into Legal Advisory and Finance Advisory — this Director role is being asked to build a "future-proof" unit inside a segment the company itself just wrote down and is dissolving as a standalone line.
Terms used on this page
KYC — Know Your Customer: verifying who a customer is before doing business with them.
CDD — Customer Due Diligence: the process of checking and monitoring a customer's identity and risk.
Wwft — Wet ter voorkoming van witwassen en financieren van terrorisme: the Dutch law that makes KYC/CDD checks mandatory. It is the Dutch version of what the EU calls anti-money laundering and counter-terrorist financing law.
UBO — Ultimate Beneficial Owner: the real person who owns or controls a company, behind any legal structure.
PEP — Politically Exposed Person: someone in a prominent public role, checked more closely for bribery and corruption risk.
FIU-Nederland — Financial Intelligence Unit Netherlands: the national body that receives unusual-transaction reports.
SEPBLAC — Spain's equivalent of FIU-Nederland: the national body that supervises anti-money laundering compliance and receives suspicious-transaction reports.
Cl@ve — Spain's government digital-ID system, aligned with the EU's electronic-identification and trust-services framework (eIDAS), used for remote identity verification.
Remote-verification vendors — the automated document + selfie/liveness providers e-commerce uses for this check: iDenfy, Trulioo, Shufti Pro, Sumsub, Regula.
FEC — Financial Economic Crime: the umbrella term compliance teams use for fraud, money laundering and related crime together.
EBITDA — Earnings Before Interest, Tax, Depreciation and Amortisation: a standard profitability measure.
FTE — Full-Time Equivalent: a standardised way of counting headcount.
KPI — Key Performance Indicator: a metric used to track performance against a target.
SLA — Service-Level Agreement: an agreed standard or deadline a team commits to.

03
Domain Knowledge
The KYC/CDD lifecycle — the 9 steps to know cold
#StepWhat happens
1Customer identificationCollect and verify identity from a reliable, independent source — official ID for individuals, Chamber of Commerce (KvK) data for entities.
2Beneficial ownership (UBO)For legal entities, identify the Ultimate Beneficial Owner(s) — the individuals who actually control or benefit from the customer.
3Business relationship assessmentEstablish the nature and purpose of the relationship — why this customer, this product, this pattern of activity.
4Risk classificationScore the customer against risk factors (client type, product, delivery channel, country) and assign a tier.
5Tiered due diligenceApply the matching depth of check — simplified, standard, or enhanced (see the Dutch risk tiers below).
6PEP & sanctions screeningCheck the customer and UBOs against politically-exposed-person and sanctions lists.
7Ongoing / periodic monitoringRe-review the customer and its risk profile on a set cycle, not just at onboarding.
8Transaction monitoringTrack activity throughout the relationship for patterns inconsistent with the stated business purpose.
9Reporting & recordkeepingFile an unusual/suspicious transaction report (in the Netherlands, to FIU-Nederland) when warranted; retain records for a minimum of 5 years.
General CDD lifecycle: ComplyAdvantage. Dutch-specific detail (KvK verification, FIU-Nederland reporting, 5-year retention): Belastingdienst — client due diligence under the Dutch Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act.
Risk tiers under the Dutch Anti-Money Laundering and Anti-Terrorist Financing Act
  1. Simplified — low-risk customers: governments, listed companies, other low-risk regulated entities.
  2. Regular (standard) — the default depth of check for a normal-risk customer.
  3. Enhanced — triggered by a PEP, a higher-risk country, or complex/unusually large transactions with no clear economic purpose.
Interview-ready talking points
  1. The KYC Center's job is to move customers through this lifecycle at scale — the commercial pitch is speed and consistency at each of the 9 steps, not just headcount.
  2. AI/data fits steps 1, 4 and 8 first — identity verification, risk scoring and transaction-pattern detection are the most automatable today.
  3. The market shift (Section 02) is a shift from step-1/2 volume work to step 5/6/9 judgment work — exactly why the posting asks for specialists, not junior analysts.

Domain Knowledge
Building an anti-money laundering program — the 6 institutional pillars
#PillarWhat it requires
1Risk assessmentA systematic analysis of the institution's own money-laundering and terrorist-financing exposure across clients, products, services, delivery channels and geographies — the risk appetite everything else scales against.
2Policies & proceduresWritten internal controls sized to the institution's risk profile, kept current as regulation and typologies change.
3Compliance functionAn independent officer or function accountable for administering and monitoring the program — required once daily policy is set by two or more people.
4Independent audit functionRegular, independent testing of the program's effectiveness — mandatory for institutions with 50 or more employees, or a high-risk profile.
5Employee screeningBackground checks on staff who handle client due diligence and transaction monitoring — CV verification, diploma checks, references.
6Employee trainingPeriodic, role-specific training so staff recognise red flags and know what to do with them.
Dutch institutional obligations: Jaeger Advocaten-belastingkundigen. Cross-checked against the general 5-pillar anti-money laundering compliance standard: Sanction Scanner. Client-level due diligence, monitoring, reporting and record-keeping are covered in Section 03 above — this table is the institutional layer built around it.
Interview-ready talking points
  1. The posting's own language — "own its commercial, operational and financial results" — maps directly onto pillars 2 through 6: policies, staffing, screening, training and audit-readiness are the operational build, not just the client-facing steps in Section 03.
  2. The independent audit function threshold (50+ employees, or a high-risk profile) is a natural frame: at the KYC Center's likely scale, audit-readiness is not optional — it is table stakes for every client engagement.
  3. A director who can speak to both layers — the 9-step client lifecycle in Section 03 and the 6 institutional pillars here — is talking the same language as the client's own compliance officer, not just the client's operations team.

Domain Knowledge
The total picture — how KYC, anti-money laundering, counter-terrorist financing, sanctions and fraud fit together
Financial Crime Prevention
The umbrella discipline: stopping an institution — or its clients — from being used to move, hide or benefit from the proceeds of crime, terrorist financing, sanctions evasion or fraud.
↓
Anti-money laundering, counter-terrorist financing & sanctions
Regulatory, after-the-fact: does this reasonably indicate financial crime that must be reported?
  • Customer due diligence & KYC — the identification and risk-tiering foundation.Full 9-step lifecycle: Section 03
  • Sanctions & PEP screening — checking customers and beneficial owners against sanctions and politically-exposed-person lists.Section 03, step 6
  • Transaction monitoring — reviewing activity for patterns inconsistent with the customer's stated purpose.Section 03, step 8
  • Unusual/suspicious transaction reporting — filing with FIU-Nederland when the law requires it.Section 03, step 9
  • Institutional program — risk assessment, policies, compliance and audit functions, screening and training.Full 6-pillar build: Section 04
Fraud prevention
Operational, real-time: is this transaction causing harm right now?
  • Identity verification & authentication — confirming the person transacting is who they claim to be, at the point of transaction.
  • Real-time transaction screening — stopping unauthorized or deceptive activity before it settles.
  • Account takeover & payment fraud prevention — protecting existing customers and accounts from compromise.
  • Chargeback & dispute management — the operational loss-prevention side of the same customer relationship.
↓
Where anti-money laundering and fraud converge
  • Mule networks & account farms
  • Synthetic identity schemes
  • Organized fraud rings
  • Structuring to avoid reporting thresholds
  • Sanctions violations linked to fraud proceeds
  • Terrorist financing disguised as ordinary loss
Structural relationship between customer due diligence and anti-money laundering: Thomson Reuters Legal ("know your customer checks who you're doing business with; anti-money laundering covers what they're doing with their money"). Anti-money-laundering-versus-fraud distinction and named overlap typologies: Unit21.
Interview-ready talking points
  1. The KYC Center's product (Section 01) sits entirely inside the left-hand column above — client-facing due diligence, screening and reporting are exactly what Team EIFFEL sells today.
  2. Richemont's Anti-Fraud & Payment accountability sits inside the right-hand column and the convergence zone beneath it — the fastest-growing area of financial-crime prevention industry-wide.
  3. A director who can speak to both columns — not just the KYC column the Center sells today — is speaking to where the whole industry is moving, which is exactly the "future-proof" and "AI, data and technological innovation" language in the posting itself.
