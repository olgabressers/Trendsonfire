Carrier Global: a €60M service P&L across Carrier, CIAT and Viessmann

Carrier's FY2025 group revenue fell 3% to $21.7B on soft Residential/Light Commercial demand — while aftermarket service posted a fifth straight year of double-digit growth. This role sits at that intersection: full P&L for a €60M, 200+ person service organisation across Carrier, CIAT and Viessmann aftermarket, with a double-digit growth target through 2030, in a Dutch market short on certified technicians.

Sources: Carrier Global FY2025/Q1 2026 earnings releases (ir.carrier.com), SEC filings, Trane Technologies, Johnson Controls, Daikin Europe, Vaillant Group results, ABN AMRO & Mordor Intelligence NL market research, LinkedIn job posting (provided) Updated 23 July 2026
Key conclusions
The company
$21.7B FY2025 group revenue (-3% YoY); Climate Solutions Europe segment $5.04B. Acquired Viessmann Climate Solutions for $14.2B in January 2024, adding a leading European residential/light-commercial HVAC and heat pump business to Carrier's commercial portfolio.
  • Carrier — global HVAC/climate parent, spanning commercial and residential systems, refrigeration, and fire & security across 160+ countries.
  • Viessmann — residential and light-commercial heat pump and boiler business.
  • CIAT — applied-HVAC aftermarket expertise across 20+ European countries.
The role
Full P&L (revenue, margin, overheads, EBIT — earnings before interest and taxes, i.e. operating profit — and cashflow) for Carrier, CIAT and Viessmann aftermarket service across the Netherlands and Belgium: maintenance, commissioning (system start-up), repairs, modernization (upgrades) and parts. 200+ headcount, €60M revenue, double-digit growth targets through 2030. Utrecht-based, ~40% regional travel.
Market context
The Dutch heat pump market is forecast to grow from $505M (2025) to $628M (2030). A planned 2026 mandate for hybrid heat pumps on boiler replacement was reversed in July 2024 — leaving the installer shortage (nearly 2,000 complaints in 2024) as the market's real constraint, not a regulatory floor.
Cross-industry leadership
A €150M P&L and 240+ person team across 26 European countries at Richemont, key-account engagement across 12 luxury Maisons, a Professional Doctorate in Engineering (Logistics & Supply Chain), and four years moving across four industry domains as an IBM Benelux consultant — a proven pattern of fast, structured leadership in new technical and operational environments, directly applicable to Carrier, CIAT and Viessmann aftermarket.

Role Analysis
3 priorities this role is built around
01
Integrating three service brands into one P&L
  • Three brands, one P&L: Carrier's own commercial HVAC, CIAT's applied HVAC, and Viessmann's residential/light-commercial heat pumps and boilers — added when Carrier bought Viessmann for $14.2B (Jan 2024)
  • The role's scope — maintenance, commissioning, repairs, modernization and parts across all three brands — is that integration's aftermarket expression
  • The task: turn three separate service organisations into one accountable, single-P&L operation
Source: Carrier 10-Q, Q1 2024 (Viessmann acquisition)
02
Growing headcount against a technician shortage
  • Nearly 2,000 Dutch consumer complaints were logged in 2024 over installer/technician availability — a documented capacity constraint here
  • A planned 2026 mandate for hybrid heat pumps on replacement was reversed in July 2024, when the incoming government replaced the legal requirement with subsidies rather than compulsion; the shortage remains the real constraint, independent of any regulation
Source: Mordor Intelligence, NL Heat Pump Market
03
Converting connected devices into aftermarket growth
  • "Connected devices" are HVAC units with remote sensors that let Carrier monitor performance and sell predictive-maintenance contracts instead of one-off repairs — this connected install base grew 25%+ in Q1 2026, and Carrier expects a sixth straight year of double-digit aftermarket (service) revenue growth group-wide as a result
  • Commercial HVAC orders, including data-center wins, were a major growth driver into 2026
  • Digital transformation and connected services are a named core accountability for this role
Source: Carrier Q1 2026 earnings call transcript

The pattern across both: warranty and invoice disputes are the single largest negative request type on both sides of the merger — 53% for Carrier, 58% for Viessmann BE. The difference is what happens next: Viessmann posts a public reply in 1.7 days, Carrier in 76.8. And on Viessmann's positive side, the top theme customers praise is speed of response (44%) — the exact capability Carrier's own reviewers say is missing (31% complain about reaching support).
The headline finding: Viessmann arrives inside this business with a mature, professionally-run service reputation in Belgium — 1,978 Trustpilot reviews, 4.5/5, every negative review answered within 1.7 days. Carrier has no Benelux service review presence at all, and its only corporate profile scores 3.2 with a 77-day average reply time. Viessmann Nederland's profile is unclaimed with zero reviews — in the single largest market this role owns, the group has no customer-voice channel running.

Read this before quoting the number 76.8 days is how long Carrier takes to reply to a review on Trustpilot — the work of a reputation/marketing team, not a dispatcher. It is not how long Carrier takes to answer a service call or send a technician; no public data exists for that, in Benelux or anywhere. Why this matters in the interview: the two biggest complaint themes — warranty handling and cost/invoice disputes — are process and governance problems, not technician-capacity problems. They are fixable without hiring a single extra engineer, in a market where hiring engineers is the binding constraint. Meanwhile Viessmann already runs the response discipline Carrier lacks, and Viessmann Nederland has no customer-voice channel at all. That is a concrete, evidence-backed first-90-days agenda: claim the Dutch channel, adopt Viessmann BE's response standard across the merged organisation, and attack warranty and invoice process before touching headcount.
