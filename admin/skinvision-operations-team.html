SkinVision: one operation that serves four teams and one goal

Operations at SkinVision sits directly on the clinical pathway. What a user is told, how fast a person confirms an assessment, and whether a high-risk user reaches a doctor are operational outcomes with clinical consequences. This page shows, for each colleague, where the six operational areas meet their work, the challenge at that interface, what Operations contributes, and how the work joins the common goal.

Sources: SkinVision Head of Operations role description, skinvision.com, SkinVision LinkedIn job description Updated 8 November 2026
Common goal
Make early detection accessible to everyone, and make sure a high-risk user reaches a doctor.
SafetySensitivity is a floor. Specificity improves only at unchanged sensitivity.
AccessMore high-risk results lead to a consultation, in every market and for every segment.
EfficiencyLower cost to serve per active user, with no degradation in any safety measure.

02
Chief Operating Officer
Andreas: the operating model
Andreas Vitsentzatos
Chief Operating Officer LinkedIn →
The operating model across the six areas: how they run as one process, who owns which boundary, and what the function delivers in the first year.
Where our work meets
  • All six areas, which today run semi-independently and are small in headcount and wide in subject matter
  • The annual mandate review: what the function has learned, what it should own, what it should hand over
  • Boundaries with Regulatory, Clinical, Data Science, Product and Commercial
Challenge at that interface
  • Six areas need common standards and interfaces, so that small teams cover a broad surface without dropping anything
  • Insight from the service only becomes value when it reaches Regulatory, Product and Commercial in a form they can use
  • Growth of the remit has to be argued on evidence while permanent boundaries stay untouched
What Operations brings
  • Month 3: a map of processes, tools, data flows, dependencies and gaps across the six areas, with a build, automate or outsource position
  • Month 6: one operational reporting layer across all six areas, with cuts by device, geography and age band
  • Month 12: specificity gains at unchanged sensitivity, more high-risk users reaching a doctor, and one opportunity quantified and taken forward
  • A written mandate review with proposals argued on evidence
How will you bring six areas together without adding layers?
I start with one shared dataset and one set of interfaces, so that the six areas read the same facts. Standards for intake classification, handoff times and reporting are agreed once with Regulatory and Clinical, and the reporting layer shows every area against the same measures. Tooling carries the load that headcount cannot, and I bring the COO a monthly view of service, cost and safety measures side by side.
Evidence from my record: at Richemont I directed 100+ strategic projects with stakeholders up to executive committee level, built the omni-channel cockpit that replaced reporting from separate teams with one commercial and operational view, and raised productivity 3 times through automation and process standardisation.

03
Support Engineering
Noel: tools that replace headcount
Noel
Support Engineer
The tools and integrations that carry Customer Service: helpdesk, billing platform, review tooling, reporting and the data model beneath them.
Where our work meets
  • Helpdesk and customer relationship management setup, templates, deflection and multilingual coverage
  • Integrations between the billing platform, the app and the support tools
  • Operational incidents: user communication, workarounds and the record handed to Regulatory
Challenge at that interface
  • Every contact is classified at capture as general enquiry, complaint, suspected incident or clinically urgent, and that class decides what reaches Regulatory and when
  • Entitlement failures (paying users without access, or the reverse) are the queue that most directly creates support contacts
  • Segment attributes such as device model, operating system version, age band and language have to be captured consistently before anyone can analyse them
What Operations brings
  • One classification standard built into the helpdesk fields, agreed with Regulatory, with handoff times documented
  • A tagging scheme that counts billing-driven contacts, so the cause is visible and goes to the owner of the fix
  • A shared record identifier across helpdesk, billing, review and follow-up, so records join
  • Automation and deflection prioritised by contact volume and cost, with an incident playbook that includes user communication
Where would you invest first in tooling and automation?
In the fields and identifiers that make every later step possible: intake classification, a joinable user record, and instrumentation for device, operating system, age band, market and language. Then automation on the highest-volume, lowest-risk contact types, with safety-relevant classes always routed to a person. I would sit with Noel on the integration backlog and rank it by contacts removed and by what it unlocks for reporting.
Evidence from my record: at Richemont I ran a Client Relations Centre of 240+ full-time-equivalents across 26 European markets on Salesforce, introduced AI agents that replaced manual work in customer service, anti-fraud and payment operations, and ran a General Data Protection Regulation quality framework that treated customer-data handling errors as critical compliance incidents.

04
Medical Operations
Jules: the clinical pathway in operation
Jules
Medical Operations Lead
The clinical pathway on the operations side: human review of algorithm output, and care access and follow-up after a high-risk result.
Where our work meets
  • Clinical Review Operations: calibration, inter-rater agreement, discordance review and quality sampling
  • Care Access and Follow-up: referral guidance, partner clinics, teledermatology handoffs and outcome capture
  • Reviewer decisions and rationales that flow to Data Science, and model changes that flow back as updated reviewer instruction
Challenge at that interface
  • The algorithm is tuned for high sensitivity, so human review has to recover specificity without touching the sensitivity floor
  • Reviewer capacity against turnaround commitments is a hard constraint
  • Care pathways differ by country, and many follow-up contacts are with people who have just been told they may have cancer
What Operations brings
  • A review workflow that runs to a measured target: specificity reported only alongside sensitivity held constant
  • The sensitivity floor written into the standard operating procedure, with any movement escalated at once to Clinical and Regulatory
  • A capacity model that links volumes, turnaround and cost per review
  • Market-specific navigation guidance, and consent-based outcome capture that stays on the navigation side of the line with medical advice
How would you work with a medical team without crossing into clinical decisions?
Operations generates the data, runs the process and escalates. The clinical call and the criteria stay with Clinical, and I treat that as structural. My part is the workflow around it: calibration cycles, agreement monitoring, capacity planning, and follow-up that shows how many high-risk users reached a doctor. When a finding has clinical meaning, it goes to Jules and Clinical with the evidence attached.
Evidence from my record: at Richemont I ran anti-fraud operations that combined machine-learning models with manual review, and held service-level agreement, operational performance and cost-to-serve targets across a €150M+ business.

05
Customer Success
Evi: users and partners over time
Evi
Customer Success Associate
The experience of users and partners over time: onboarding, renewal, partner support for insurers, employers and health systems, and the voice of the user.
Where our work meets
  • Business-to-business partner support and invoicing, reconciled against contract terms
  • Billing and renewal handling: subscriptions, trials, cancellations and payment failure
  • Churn drivers, packaging friction and pricing questions that show up in support and billing data
Challenge at that interface
  • Signals such as pricing sensitivity, churn and entitlement friction appear in operational data before they appear in a revenue forecast
  • Partner invoicing disputes take time when usage and seat counts are not reconciled against contract terms
  • Users who receive a high-risk result need clear, empathetic communication and a next step
What Operations brings
  • One view of a user across support, billing, review and follow-up, cut by segment
  • Billing-driven contacts tagged by cause, with findings taken to Product and Commercial as evidence
  • Partner reporting built on the same data, with invoicing reconciled to contract terms
  • Renewal protection through dunning and settlement handling, so a failed payment does not remove access from a paying user
What would you do first for customers and partners?
Make the picture of the customer joined up. I would bring support, billing and follow-up data into one view, start with the three or four contact reasons that drive most volume, and trace each to its owner. For partners, I would put usage, seats and invoices side by side with contract terms so disputes close faster. Evi and I would agree which signals go to Product and Commercial, and in which form.
Evidence from my record: at Richemont I turned the Client Relations Centre from a cost centre into a commercial e-boutique channel that drove direct sales, led the digital customer journey for 12 Maisons across 26 countries, and improved Net Promoter Score and overall satisfaction by up to 20%.

Working session
Four topics to work through with Jules and Evi
1
The journey after a high-risk result
Map each step from result to consultation, in one market, and place a measure and an owner at each step.
Measure: high-risk result to consultation conversion, time from result to consultation.
2
The intake classification standard
Draft the four contact classes and the handoff rule for each, ready to agree with Regulatory.
Measure: classification accuracy at intake, time to handoff.
3
One view of a user
List what support, billing, review and follow-up each know about the same user, and the identifier that joins them.
Measure: joinability of records across areas.
4
Cost to serve with safety held
Rank the highest-volume contact reasons, and choose which ones to remove at source, automate or keep with a person.
Measure: contacts per active user, cost to serve, with every safety measure reported beside it.
