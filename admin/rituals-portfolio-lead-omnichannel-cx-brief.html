Interview Brief — Portfolio Lead, Omnichannel Customer Experience (CX) · Digital

Rituals: €2.43 billion in revenue, 1,500 boutiques mid-refit, one Digital organization asking for a shared view

Founded on Amsterdam's Kalverstraat in 2000 by Raymond Cloosterman, Rituals passed €2.43 billion in net revenue in 2025, up 16% year-on-year, across nearly 1,500 boutiques and more than 4,500 department-store and perfumery points of sale in 33 countries. It became a certified B Corporation in 2023 and, in June 2026, was named among the first nine companies worldwide certified under B Lab's tightened new standards. The Digital organization spans web, app, point of sale, loyalty, customer service, content, customer relationship management, data, platforms, architecture and delivery — and is mid-way through a €40 million, 30-working-day refit of all 1,500 boutiques. This role is the one asked to give that organization one shared view of its priorities.

Sources: rituals.com newsroom, careers.rituals.com, Companies House (UK), B Lab / Pioneers Post, TheIndustry.beauty Updated 24 September 2026

Key conclusions
The company
Founded in Amsterdam in 2000 by Raymond Cloosterman, a former Unilever executive. Privately held and founder-controlled. Passed €2.43 billion in net revenue in 2025 (+16% year-on-year), with an EBITDA margin of roughly 22.5%, across nearly 1,500 boutiques and 4,500+ wholesale points of sale in 33 countries, with 12,000+ employees worldwide. Certified B Corporation since 2023; recertified in June 2026 as one of the first nine companies globally under B Lab's stricter new standards.
The role
Portfolio Lead, Omnichannel Customer Experience (CX) — based at the Central Head Office (CHO) on Herengracht, Amsterdam, 32–40 hours a week, inside the Digital organization. Owns portfolio governance, decision rhythm and intake standards across a Digital function spanning web, app, point of sale, loyalty, customer service, content, customer relationship management, data, platforms, architecture and delivery, working in a matrix with Trading, Marketing & Customer Relationship Management, Content & Design, Product, Architecture, Customer Relations, Insights, Marketplaces, Data and the separate Enterprise Portfolio function.
Company context
Rituals is mid-way through a €40 million refit of all roughly 1,500 boutiques across 30 countries, completed in 30 working days between 3 August and 25 September 2026, at a pace of more than 40 boutiques a day using around 100 teams — with a matching Asia and Middle East refit planned for the first quarter of 2027. Online sales already exceed 20% of revenue.
Key opportunity
A Digital organization built from ten-plus disciplines and a growing list of open product and portfolio roles is being asked, in its own words, to bring "structure without creating bureaucracy." This role is the one built to answer that, at the exact moment the company's largest physical operation in years is running through the same organization.

Company, ownership & the beauty and wellbeing retail market
Founding story
Thirteen years at Unilever, then a basement on an Amsterdam canal

Raymond Cloosterman spent thirteen years at Unilever before taking time off to travel and study customs and daily rituals across Asia. In 2000, at 36, he opened the first Rituals store on Amsterdam's Kalverstraat, working with a small team that included an anthropologist and perfumers from Paris to turn everyday products — shower gels, shaving foams, home fragrance — into a wellbeing experience rather than a commodity purchase.

Sources: Harper's Bazaar Malaysia, founder interview · careers.rituals.com, company timeline

B Corporation certification
Certified twice — once under the original standard, once under B Lab's tightened 2026 standard

Rituals first achieved B Corporation certification in 2023. In June 2026 it was named among the first nine companies worldwide — alongside Ricola, Lumene, Bolsius and five others — certified under B Lab's new standards, introduced in April 2025, which require minimum performance across seven social and environmental criteria (including climate action, human rights and fair work) rather than the previous points-based system that allowed strong performance in one area to offset weak performance in another.

Source: Pioneers Post, 17 June 2026

Key Performance Indicator (KPI) framework — four layers
Portfolio governance & predictability
Program Increment (PI) objective achievement rate
Share of committed Program Increment objectives Digital actually delivers, once one shared portfolio cadence exists.
Benchmark: the Scaled Agile Framework (SAFe) community treats 80%+ predictability as the mature target; newly formed Agile Release Trains typically start in the 40–60% range and improve to 80%+ within three to four Program Increments.
On-time, on-budget delivery rate
Share of portfolio initiatives landing inside their committed schedule and budget.
Benchmark: Project Management Institute Pulse of the Profession research found only 34% of organizations deliver on time and within budget; mature portfolio management offices benchmark 80%+ on-time delivery.
Delivery commitment reliability
Consistency of what Digital commits to each cycle against what it actually delivers.
Benchmark: mature, stabilized teams sustain 80–90% commitment reliability, with a coefficient of variation in delivered output of 0.20–0.35 or lower.
Portfolio dependency & risk transparency
How much of the active portfolio's dependencies, risks and capacity constraints are visible in one place rather than scattered by team.
Benchmark: no independently verified external figure exists for this measure; it depends on the governance tool and cadence this role is asked to design.
