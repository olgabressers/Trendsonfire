Interview Brief — Head of Operations, EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa) · Fulfilment

Nivoda: $1 billion in diamond sales, three fulfilment hubs to run as one

Founded in London in 2017, Nivoda has raised $96 million and built a business-to-business marketplace connecting 10,000+ retailers across 70+ countries to 2 million+ diamonds and gemstones. It passed $1.04 billion in cumulative diamond sales in March 2026 and is expanding into finished jewellery the same year. Fulfilment for the region runs across three hubs — the Netherlands, the United Kingdom and South Africa — each with its own carrier network and no single owner today. This role is that owner.

Key conclusions
The company
A London-founded, venture-backed business-to-business marketplace for diamonds and gemstones — 2 million+ stones, 10,000+ retailers, 70+ countries, $96 million raised, roughly 427 employees across five continents. Passed $1.04 billion in cumulative diamond sales in March 2026.
The role
Head of Operations, EMEA (Europe, Middle East and Africa) — on-site in Oude Meer, Netherlands, reporting to the Vice President of Fulfillment. Owns fulfilment performance — speed, accuracy, service-level agreements — across three hubs: the Netherlands, the United Kingdom and South Africa. Direct oversight of the United Kingdom and South Africa teams, plus carrier relationships with FedEx, UPS and Brinks.
Company context
Series C closed November 2024 ($51 million, led by Northzone). A further $60 million non-dilutive trade-credit facility followed in September 2025. 2026 brought a Bangkok office, a gemstone-marketplace relaunch, and a first move into finished jewellery — growth is adding operational complexity on top of the same three hubs.
Key opportunity
Three hubs, three sets of habits, one shared job to do — unify intake, quality control, shipping and returns into one performance standard before the next billion dollars of sales and the finished-jewellery launch land on the same network.

Founding story
Two founders, one sourcing problem

David Sutton, Nivoda's co-founder and chief executive officer, started in the diamond trade in South Africa, founded his own diamond wholesale business, Sutton Diamonds, in 2007, and then co-founded the bridal jewellery brand Taylor & Hart in 2012. Sourcing diamonds for that business exposed how fragmented and local the trade still was. In 2017 he founded Nivoda in London with Andre Woons, now the company's co-founder and chief information officer, to build a single digital marketplace connecting suppliers directly to retailers.

Sources: David Sutton, LinkedIn · Andre Woons, Crunchbase · Companies House (incorporated 24 April 2017, originally as World Gem Centre Ltd)

Competitive landscape
The established business-to-business diamond trading platforms
RapNet
Run by Rapaport since 1996 — the trade's oldest network. Natural diamonds only; deepest listings and broadest international reach.
IDEX
International Diamond Exchange Online, founded 2000, rooted in the Israeli and Antwerp trade. More locally concentrated than RapNet or Nivoda.
VDB (Virtual Diamond Boutique)
Natural and lab-grown, like Nivoda. Strong on visual, mobile-first sales tools; less integrated logistics.
Nivoda's stated differentiation: a single checkout across natural diamonds, lab-grown diamonds and colored gemstones, zero-inventory sourcing for retailers, and embedded buyer financing through Nivoda Capital.
Role Analysis
3 structural challenges this role is built to solve
01
Three hubs, no shared standard
  • The Netherlands, United Kingdom and South Africa hubs run fulfilment today without one performance benchmark
  • The role's own brief asks for "consistent standards and service-level agreements regionally" — meaning they don't exist yet
  • Intake, quality control, shipping and returns each need unifying into one operating model
02
High-value goods, a nine-carrier network
  • Nivoda's own shipping terms name nine courier partners globally; the Europe, Middle East and Africa hubs run on three of them — FedEx, UPS and Brinks
  • Every return requires unedited video evidence of the sealed parcel, then a second video of it being opened — loose diamonds and gemstones carry authentication risk an ordinary parcel doesn't
03
Growth landing on the same footprint
  • The platform passed $1.04 billion in cumulative diamond sales in March 2026 and expects the next billion within roughly a year
  • 2026 also brought the first move into finished jewellery — new product complexity arriving on the same three hubs, with no headcount increase named in the role

KPIs
KPI (Key Performance Indicator) framework — four layers
Fulfilment performance
Speed
Intake-to-ship cycle time across all three hubs — the first of three fulfilment measures the role owns, named directly in the posting.
Industry benchmark: order-to-ship in 24–48 hours is the standard target; leading operators push to same-day dispatch (under 12 hours) for orders placed before the daily cutoff.
Accuracy
Order and quality-control accuracy across intake, picking and dispatch — the second of the three named measures.
Industry benchmark: pick accuracy averages 97–98% industry-wide, with best-in-class fulfilment operations reaching 99.5–99.9%.
Service-level agreements
Adherence to the consistent regional standards this role is explicitly asked to establish.
Industry benchmark: 95%+ compliance is the standard target across order accuracy, shipping time and inventory service-level agreements.
Returns cycle time
Time from a return being initiated to its authenticated resolution, under Nivoda's own quality-check process.
Industry benchmark: leading warehouses resolve returns — inspection through disposition — within 24–48 hours of receipt; the broader industry standard is under 5 business days.
Benchmarks are general fulfilment and warehouse-industry standards, not figures disclosed by Nivoda. Sources: DCL Logistics, 3PL KPI benchmarks · ShipDudes, fulfilment industry standards · KPI Depot, SLA compliance rate · DCL Logistics, returns management benchmarks
Carrier & logistics network
Carrier cost per shipment
FedEx, UPS and Brinks cost, benchmarked across the three hubs — carrier relationship management is named directly in the role.
Industry benchmark: insured high-value shipments via armored/specialty carriers typically run 1–2% of declared value once insurance and last-mile security are added.
Carrier on-time rate
% of shipments delivered within each carrier's own committed window, tracked hub by hub.
Industry benchmark: 95–98% on-time delivery is the standard target range for premium/express logistics providers.
Third-party logistics evaluation
Ongoing assessment of external providers against cost, speed and reliability — 3PL (third-party logistics) familiarity is named as a nice-to-have in the posting.
Industry benchmark: 99.5%+ order accuracy; Perfect Order Rate above 90% (APQC industry median), with top performers exceeding 95%.
Authentication & loss rate
Returns passing quality and authenticity checks versus flagged for investigation — the risk profile of shipping loose diamonds and gemstones.
Industry benchmark: jewelry businesses report theft rates roughly 60% higher than the average retailer, with US jewelry theft losses estimated at $1.2 billion a year (FBI data).
Benchmarks are general logistics, 3PL and jewelry-trade industry standards, not figures disclosed by Nivoda. Sources: Shippo, shipping high-value jewelry (2026) · DCL Logistics, 3PL KPI benchmarks · Red Stag Fulfillment, 3PL performance metrics · CaratTrade, jewelry theft statistics (FBI data)
