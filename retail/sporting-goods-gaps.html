Where Sporting Goods Brands Are Losing — and Where the White Space Is

A strategic gap analysis across sporting goods brands: heritage sport, premium performance, outdoor, digital-native and value players. Direct-to-consumer revenue disclosures, market context, and 9 white space opportunities. Updated 13 August 2026.

Updated 13 August 2026 Scope: Global strategy + European market context Sources: named public company filings and press releases, linked inline
Sporting goods equipment price inflation across the EU slowed to 1.0% in 2024, down from a peak of 3.8% in 2022 (Eurostat, April 2025), giving value-conscious consumers more room to switch brands. Against that backdrop, challenger brands have grown quickly by filling specific unmet needs. Lululemon reached $10.6B in net revenue in fiscal 2024 (lululemon athletica, 27 March 2025). Gymshark reached £556.2M in revenue for the year to July 2023, per accounts filed with Companies House (BusinessCloud, reporting on the filed accounts). On Running grew net sales from CHF 724.6M in 2021 to CHF 2,318.3M in 2024 — roughly 3.2× in three years (On Holding AG, full-year 2024 results). Meanwhile, the mass of heritage brands — Champion, Reebok, Fila, Puma — compete on the same axes: logo, price promotion, and athlete licensing. The window to claim unoccupied territory is open.
Sources: adidas Annual Report 2024; NIKE, Inc. Q4 & Full Year Fiscal 2024 results; On Holding AG full-year 2024 results; Eurostat, April 2025. Links inline on each figure below.

The Macro Environment — Why the Window Is Open Now

EU price inflation for sport, camping and open-air recreation equipment slowed to 1.0% in 2024, down from a peak of 3.8% in 2022, after two years of deceleration (Eurostat, April 2025). Slower price growth gives consumers more room to compare brands on value rather than absorbing higher costs at incumbent retailers.

Secondhand and resale platforms have expanded into the category. Vinted, Europe's largest online resale marketplace, grew gross merchandise value 47% year-on-year to €10.8B in 2025 and named "sports" as one of the consumer-goods categories it expanded into (Vinted newsroom, 2025 financial results).

At the same time, community-positioned challenger brands have grown quickly: On Running's net sales rose from CHF 724.6M (2021) to CHF 2,318.3M (2024), and Lululemon passed $10.6B in net revenue in fiscal 2024 (sources cited above). Both compete directly with heritage sporting goods brands for the same consumer wallet.

Methodology & Sources

Every figure in this report is drawn from a named public source — a company's own annual report, investor results release, or regulatory filing (Companies House, Eurostat) — linked inline at the point where it is used. Where a comparable public disclosure does not exist for a brand or metric, that brand or metric is not shown, rather than estimated.

  • Direct-to-consumer revenue share is shown only for the three companies that publish this split in their own financial disclosures: Nike, adidas, and On Holding.
  • Store counts, category coverage, and capability comparisons across all 18 brands have been removed from this report — a reliable, publicly verifiable source does not exist for cross-brand capability scoring of this kind.
  • White space opportunities are strategic hypotheses, built on the named, sourced brand initiatives and revenue figures cited throughout this report.
