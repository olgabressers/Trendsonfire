Europe's Travel Demand Hits a Record 82% — While Airfares Climb 26.5% in a Year

Fresh EU, US and Dutch travel data show two forces colliding in 2026 — the highest European travel intent since 2020, and the steepest one-year jump in flight costs in recent memory.

Sources: NerdWallet, Deloitte, ETC, Eurostat, ANWB, CBS, MarktEffect, Belastingdienst, Airbnb (linked inline) Updated 27 July 2026

Europeans are traveling in record numbers, and paying record prices to do it. 82% of Europeans plan to travel between April and September 2026 — the highest share since 2020 — while US airfares rose 26.5% in the year to June 2026 and overall US travel costs are up 10% versus last summer. In the Netherlands, the travel trade association ANVR estimates this summer's package holidays cost 7–12% more than in 2025, and the government's flight ticket tax rose again on 1 January 2026.

The Real Dutch Income Split
CBS's own income-quartile breakdown of summer 2025 vacation participation — the most recent official Dutch figures available, published 3 July 2026.

The richest 25% of Dutch households take a summer holiday twice as often as the poorest 25%. CBS data shows 87.3% of people in the top income quartile went on summer holiday in 2025, against 41.4% in the bottom quartile — a 46-point gap. The third quartile sits at 74.3%, the second at 58.7%. Among those who do travel, low- and high-income households go equally often — an average of two trips each — so the gap is not about frequency once you can afford to go. It is about whether you go at all.

Source: CBS, "Bijna 22 miljoen zomervakanties in 2025, Frankrijk populairste bestemming", published 3 July 2026. Overall, 72% of people aged 15+ in the Netherlands took a summer holiday in 2025 — a national average that hides the quartile-level gap above.
